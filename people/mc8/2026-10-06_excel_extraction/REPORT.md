# Excel extraction v1 results

## Result

The canonical extractor passed every check on the committed synthetic fixtures.

| Evaluation | Workbook | Passed | Accuracy |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Level 1 reconstruction | `edge_cases.xlsx` | 33/33 | 100% |
| Level 1 reconstruction | `gold_financial_statement.xlsx` | 28/28 | 100% |
| Level 2 line-item survival | `gold_financial_statement.xlsx` | 5/5 records | 100% |
| Level 2 coordinate fidelity | `gold_financial_statement.xlsx` | 5/5 records | 100% |
| Level 2 value fidelity | `gold_financial_statement.xlsx` | 10/10 values | 100% |

The edge-case fixture verifies formula text, one present and one missing formula
cache, merged anchors and children, hidden rows and columns, a hidden sheet,
error cells, booleans, dates, number formats, multiple tables, titles, and
footnotes. The gold workbook contains separate balance-sheet and profit-and-loss
sheets with hand-labeled raw and formula-derived values.

## Interpretation

The Level 1 result shows that the JSON representation can reconstruct each
detected table without changing raw cell values or cached formula values. It is
primarily a serialization regression test.

The Level 2 result shows that the selected financial line items, values, and
coordinates survive the parser on the small gold fixture. It does not establish
performance on sponsor workbooks, unusual Excel layouts, or financial semantic
normalization. Those claims require a representative real test set.

Machine-readable results are in `results/evaluation.json`. The fixtures are
deterministically generated by `scripts/generate_fixtures.py` and contain no
restricted data.
