1 / Research question

Can a parser’s confidence tell us when to trust its output?

The hypothesis: higher confidence from the model should correspond to more accurate transcription.

2 / Where confidence comes from

Each model exposes a different signal

Tesseract

One score per recognized word

The engine converts internal word certainty c to a 0–100 score. We rescale it to 0–1.

s = clip(1 + c/20, 0, 1)

Rationale: stronger recognition certainty should indicate fewer word errors.

Engine-specific certainty. PyMuPDF renders the page; Tesseract recognizes the words.

PaddleOCR

One score per recognized line

Average the selected character probabilities after removing decoder blanks and duplicates.

s = mean(pcharacter)

Rationale: consistently confident character choices should indicate a reliable line.

PP-OCRv5 mobile. Averaging may hide uncertain characters.

MinerU recognition

One probability per subword token

Apply softmax to decoder logits and read the probability of the emitted token.

pt = exp(zt) / ∑k exp(zk)

Rationale: a more concentrated next-token prediction indicates greater model certainty.

Depends on image and preceding output. Full-page recognition only, excluding layout parsing.

Scores come from model inference, with no prompt asking the model to rate itself.

Native score scales differ across engines. A value of 0.95 does not yet establish 95% correctness.

3 / From local scores to a document

Two summaries encode different assumptions

Character-weighted mean

C = ∑ wisi / ∑ wi

Give longer output units more influence. Here, w is the number of characters in a word, line or decoded token.

Reason to try it: summarize confidence over the amount of text produced.

Geometric mean

G = exp(mean(log si))

Give low scores a stronger effect than an arithmetic average. Each output unit has equal weight.

Reason to try it: favor consistently confident output. A zero score makes this summary zero.

Illustration with equal-length units: 0.99, 0.99, 0.20
Weighted mean: 0.727     Geometric mean: 0.581

These are candidate document-quality indicators. Their relationship to actual correctness must be measured.

Products shrink with document length. These averages can miss omissions and over-weight repetitive output.

6 / Research conclusion

Native confidence needs validation against correctness

  1. Define the target: transcription accuracy.Review representative source pages, including numbers, difficult scans, omissions and the observed repetition failures.
  2. Test aggregation on separate documents.Choose the summary before evaluating held-out documents or issuers. Check whether higher confidence reliably ranks better output.
  3. Calibrate the magnitude only after that test.Fit a mapping from native scores to observed accuracy on a calibration set. Then test whether a predicted 95% corresponds to roughly 95% accuracy on unseen data.

Current conclusion: native confidence is available and sometimes informative. The prototype has not yet established a calibrated document-level correctness score.

Evidence and scope
  • Measured data: six documents from five issuers, 140 pages per engine, 13 assistant-authored draft snippets. Accuracy uses best-matching normalized text windows, which can be optimistic. No human-reviewed full-document references were available.
  • Full measured results and repetition source checks.
  • Tesseract confidence conversion; PaddleOCR character-score aggregation; MLX decoder probabilities.
  • Models: Tesseract 5.5.3; PP-OCRv5 mobile; MinerU2.5-Pro-2605-1.2B. The prototype does not test table structure or field associations.
